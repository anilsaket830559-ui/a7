Windbreaker Jackets Explained

A windbreaker is a thin, light jacket built to stop wind. It is the easiest layer to carry and often the one you reach for most.

  • 5 sections
  • 6 min read
  • Reviewed Nov 2026
01

What a windbreaker does

A tightly woven outer fabric blocks moving air, so the warm layer around your body is not blown away. That alone can make you feel several degrees warmer on a breezy day, without the bulk of a heavy coat.

02

Fabrics to look for

Most windbreakers use nylon or polyester.

  • Ripstop weaves have a grid of stronger threads that stops tears spreading.
  • Water-repellent finishes make light rain bead off.
  • Mesh linings help heat escape when you are active.
03

Windbreaker or waterproof?

A windbreaker breathes well and copes with a light shower, but it is not fully waterproof. For steady rain you need a shell with taped seams and a waterproof membrane. Many people carry a windbreaker most days and save the shell for wet forecasts.

04

Features that matter

A hood with a drawcord stays up in a gust. Elastic or adjustable cuffs and a hem drawcord seal out draughts. A full-length zip lets you vent heat, and a jacket that packs into its own pocket is easy to keep in a bag.

05

Getting the fit right

Leave room for a light fleece underneath, and check that the sleeves still cover your wrists when you reach forward. The back should cover your waist when you bend. Too tight and it restricts movement; too loose and it flaps in the wind.
